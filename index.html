Greetings fellow library lover!


Thank you for visiting this, my humble e-abode.

I maintain this page to share my thoughts and ideas, because that's what libraries do best and it is my personal mission to ensure the public library remains a strong and central component of my community's way of life. I desire this, not just because because I want to maintain the historical thing that is a public library for historical continuity only, but because I sincerely believe that public libraries actually hold the future for a polite and civil society. If you share this goal for your community, please read on.

To put it succinctly, I believe that the misinformation, misanthropy, and loneliness prevalent in our society today are directly caused by our over-dependence on algorithm-fed, bot-spammed, digital communities. Somewhere along the way, information as a thing that we referenced and then sought to apply in the geographic community we share with other people morphed into a place we became absorbed into at the price of real human interaction. This is not an luddite anti-technology screed (you found me on the internet didn't you?), nor an absolute denunciation in all cases. Obviously those isolated by infirmity or other limitations can derive great benefit from video chats such as those made possible by technologies such as Zoom, Teams, and Webex among many others. Indeed, telehealth is bringing healthcare accessibility to those for whom transportation has been a significant barrier. What exactly am I getting at, then? The digital world must be seen as supplementary to the community we phystically live in, not a complete replacement. Face-to-face human interaction is irreplaceable.

What's the big deal?

When two people speak to one another face-to-face, both parties must take in the full impact of what is said upon each other. Online, it is very easy to say terrible things and never have to deal with the pain it causes others. In fact, the algorithms promote whatever is most salacious, most outrageous and generates the most comments or posts. This is referred to as "engagements", and only one thing matters with "engagements": quantity. More people interacting means more time using the app or website and this is directly monetized by serving up advertisements. We have seen the bad behaviour encouraged online spill back into the communities in which we live, with people attacking others or performing dangerous acts for content. Others record terrible acts occurring in real-time or stare as though consuming content rather than intervening as was once the norm. Our emotions are being preyed upon for ad revenue as we are slowly turned into reactionary "live-wires" on edge and bristling to unload a keyboard manifesto in person, not to increase understanding or gain consensus, but to do the most harm in the way that gets the most "likes".

We must place greater emphasis on internal validation and be less easily swayed, less easily controlled. Libraries are great reunifiers. There are few places we go without a specific to-do list in place, and the library may be one of those as we go to just browse. This creates opportunities for people who might otherwise have never come across each other to connect. Left to our own devices we tend to circulate in places that people in similar social or economic strata congregate. The library is for all, thus presenting the most accurate and accessible microcosm of the community at large we occupy. This is relevant in all but the smallest of communities. These chance connections may lead to new acquaintences or friendships, but perhaps more importantly, they build awareness and empathy for all aspects of the community in which we live. This connectedness, this access to each other, is the critical role the library must play bringing to its visitors the concept of the community as collection. Here the library staff curate events and classes to draw in segments of the community both for the purposes of novel conenction as well as for innovative interaction that could result in anything from a new business venture, a new charity, or a new advocacy group to neighborhood watch or clean-up groups. Executed correctly, the library is a bastion of empowering connectedness.
